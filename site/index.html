Walking around a VAE's latent space

A variational autoencoder with a 2-D latent, trained on MNIST. Every point of the plane is a code z; the decoder turns it into an image, right here in your browser. The β slider swaps in models trained with a stronger or weaker pull toward the prior N(0, I).

Things to try

  • Drag slowly across the plane at β = 1. Digits morph into each other: the latent space is a continuous map, and nearby codes decode to similar images.
  • Switch the background to decoded grid to see the whole map at once.
  • Click the digits under the plane to hide or show their codes; double-click one to see it alone. Try just 4 and 9, or 3, 5 and 8: they overlap, and that overlap is where the reconstructions below mix them up. The selection stays as you move β, so you can watch one class's region shrink toward the centre.
  • The images show the decoder's output p(x|z): for each pixel, the probability that it is on. That is why they are grey at the edges. Switch pixels to sampled to see what the model actually generates, x ~ Bernoulli(p), and press resample for another draw.
  • At small β, digits are sharp, but the codes (scatter) spread out in clusters with gaps. Drag into a gap inside the 86% circle: the image there is often a blur no digit owns.
  • Raise β. Reconstructions get blurrier (recon ↑) as the codes are squeezed toward the prior (KL ↓), but the prior samples at the bottom more often look like real digits. More of the prior's mass decodes to something plausible.
  • Under the prior samples, the same ten test digits are reconstructed by every β model. Each row encodes the image with that model's own encoder and decodes its μ(x), so it compares the models themselves, not whatever a fixed point of the plane happens to mean in each one. Read down a column: at β = 16 strokes blur and digits slide toward a generic shape. With only two latent dimensions every model also confuses some classes (4 and 9, 3, 5 and 8), even at small β. One such miss outweighs the blur in a ten-image score, so each row shows its score on these images next to the test-set average. That average is flat up to β = 1 and climbs after it.
  • The training curves at the bottom show recon and KL per epoch for every β at once. Hover for the values at an epoch, click a legend chip to hide a β, or click a line to load that model.
  • Below them, losses vs β lays the same runs side by side. Each model was trained on recon + β·KL, but the third chart scores all of them on plain recon + KL, the negative ELBO, which bounds −log p(x). On the test set it is lowest at β = 1, the original VAE objective: that is the one β that optimises the bound itself. Any other β gives up some of the bound for something else. Small β buys sharper reconstructions (recon ↓, KL ↑); large β buys better coverage of the prior (KL ↓, so samples from N(0, I) land where the decoder has seen codes). Press ▶ to replay training and watch the trade-off form.
  • At the largest β, whole digit classes vanish: the model gives up on information to match the prior.
